## ⚡ 2. SHORT — "The Secret" (~45–60s)  ⭐ best all-rounder

> **Hook (0–3s):** "Your phone charger has a secret it never tells you."

Watch this — the power jumps… then quietly changes.
Your charger is switching modes, all by itself.
Here's why.
At first it pushes a steady current — fast charging — and the voltage climbs.
But push too hard and the battery overheats.
So the chip watches the voltage… checking, checking…
until it hits 4.2 volts.
And *snap* — it flips to constant voltage, and the current fades out gently.
That switch? It happens every single time you plug in.
You just never knew it was there.

> **End card:** "Follow for the stuff inside your electronics."

- **Title ideas:** `Your charger has a SECRET 🔋` · `Why your phone charges in 2 modes` · `The hidden switch inside every charger`

## 🔥 3. SHORT — "The Danger" (~30s)

> **Hook:** "This is why your phone doesn't explode while charging."

Push too much current into a battery… it gets hot.
Too much voltage… it can swell and get damaged.
So a tiny chip steps in.
It caps the current, watches the voltage,
and the instant it's full enough — it backs off automatically.
A little bodyguard you never see.

> **End card:** "More electronics secrets → follow."

- **Title ideas:** `Why your battery doesn't explode 🔥` · `The chip that protects your battery`

## 🎯 4. SHORT — "CC vs CV in 30s" (~30s)

> **Hook:** "Charging happens in 2 secret modes. Here they are."

Mode one — Constant Current.
Steady current, voltage climbing. This is the fast part.
Mode two — Constant Voltage.
Voltage held steady, current fading to zero. The gentle finish.
The switch happens at about 4.2 volts.
Now you know what your charger's actually doing.

> **End card:** "Follow for 60-second electronics."

- **Title ideas:** `CC vs CV in 30 seconds` · `2 modes your charger uses`

## 🔬 5. SHORT — "Smaller than your fingernail" (~30s)

Inside your charger is a chip smaller than your fingernail.
It reads the voltage. It feels the heat.
It decides how much power to send…
and switches it thousands of times a second.
Chips like the TP4056 do this in almost everything you own.
A whole brain… on a speck of silicon.
